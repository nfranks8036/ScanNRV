## Lingo and jargon
Police, fire, and rescue use more than just ten codes, sometimes they use jargon and specific phrases that aid with efficient communication over the radio. If you hear something over the radio that's not on this list, ask about it in #radio-chat! If someone knows the answer, it may get added to this list.

__**Other Codes & Abbreviations**__ <br>
`Code 3` - Respond with lights / sirens <br>
`Code 4` - Situation under control / secure / no further assistance is required <br>
`Code Gray` - Deceased without life-saving efforts (i.e., dead on arrival) <br>
`P/F/R` - **P**olice/**f**ire/**r**escue, not usually said over the radio but can be used as an abbreviation in text <br>
`UTL` - **U**nable **T**o **L**ocate, indicating responders cannot find the patient or scene <br>
`RP` - **R**eporting **P**arty, this is the individual who made the 911 call or report <br>
`RO` - **R**egistered **O**wner, this is the individual who owns a certain piece of property, vehicle, or land <br>
`BOLO` (or `BOL` or `BLL`) - **B**e **O**n the **L**ook**O**ut <br>
`EtOH` - Chemical shorthand for ethyl alcohol, often meaning alcohol is involved or a subject is intoxicated <br>
`BLS`/`ALS` - **B**asic vs **A**dvanced **L**ife **S**upport in EMS, this is the level of care provided ("Rescue" is BLS, "Medic" is ALS). ([more](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/bls-training/difference-between-als-and-bls/)) <br>
`CAD` - **C**omputer **A**ided **D**ispatch, the software used by dispatchers ([more](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/publications/CAD_TN_0911-508.pdf)) <br>
`EMD` - **E**mergency **M**edical **D**ispatch, see [the EMS priorities](https://discord.com/channels/862145376646856725/1199736805168259254/1447753171194871930) section for more info <br>
`NOL` - **N**ext **O**n **L**ist, call next tow truck in the rotation to ensure fair distribution of calls for tow companies <br>
`EDP` - **E**motionally **D**isturbed **P**erson, this is a call type typically involving a mental health crisis or emotional distress <br>
`MDT` - **M**obile **D**ata **T**erminal, the computer police have to show calls, people, and incident info <br>

__**Words & Phrases**__ <br>
`Clear` - Finished with call/transmission. P/F/R use it to indicate finished with a call, BT uses it to indicate transmission over. <br>
`Staging` - When a unit is waiting at a safe location that is a safe distance away for some hazard on-scene to be cleared. <br>
`Break` - Essentially saying you're ending your last transmission and beginning your next one <br>
`Life-Guard` - Local air ambulance service provided by Carilion. ([more](<https://www.carilionclinic.org/health-and-wellness/article/behind-scenes-look-life-guard>)) <br>
`Side Alpha`/`Bravo`/`Charlie`/`Delta` - Quick ways to identify a specific side of a building, see visual [here](<https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcT5uebb7-KjPRrwf1fyHNHj95MWb8NkzybAqQ&s>) <br>
`Emergent` - Lights & sirens <br>
`Non-Emergent` - No lights or sirens <br>
`Co-responder` (or `Co-response` or `CR`) - Mental health provider that rides with police to respond to calls <br>
`Command` - Person in charge of an incident ("Incident Commander"), often will identify over radio as "command". <br>

__**Person Descriptions**__ <br>
- Sometimes police use physical descriptors to identify a subject, but sometimes they simplify it down to race and sex. While this system is going away, it's still used here in Montgomery County. This is what they mean: <br>
    - **Race:** `A` Asian ("Alpha"), `B` Black ("Bravo"), `H` Hispanic ("Hotel"), `W` White ("Whiskey") <br>
  - **Sex:** `M` Male ("Mike"), `F` Female ("Foxtrot") <br>
- **Format over Radio:** `Race`/`Sex` (e.g., "`W`hiskey/`M`ike" = White Male, "`A`lpha/`F`oxtrot" = Asian Female) <br>
