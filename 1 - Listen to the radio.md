# Listening to the Radio - NRV911
## How does the radio work?
Unlike a traditional radio scanner, this radio system does not just listen to one channel at a time - it listens to *many* at the exact same time.

A special radio receiver with multiple antennas is connected to a laptop constantly capturing transmissions from multiple frequencies. These audio clips are streamed to different services that you may be familiar with (OpenMHz, RdioScanner, Broadcastify, etc.) where they are processed.

Every time a transmission is captured, the system saves the audio as a sound bite and places it into a queue. Because the system monitors many different channels simultaneously, multiple of these sound bites can arrive at the exact same time. This means that during particularly busy times, the queue can grow very quickly.

After these audio bites are added to the queue, the system will then play each snippet back in the order in which it was received, allowing you to hear every transmission without missing anything--even if it occurred at the same time.

## Radio quality
The radio receiver is stationed in **Christiansburg, VA** as of August 24, 2026. This means that certain towers that are more distant from this location may have a weaker sound to them and may be harder to understand (such as Blacksburg Fire, Blacksburg Rescue, and Virginia Tech Rescue).

The New River Valley uses analogue radio, which is more susceptible to background noise than other forms of radio. Blacksburg Transit, on the other hand, uses a form of digital radio (called "DMR"), which is less susceptible to background noise and interference. If anyone has any questions about the specifics about how this system is set-up, send a direct message to Noah Franks from Firewatch (contact via Discord preferred).
